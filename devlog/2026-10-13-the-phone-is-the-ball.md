# The phone is the ball

*13 October 2026 · day one*

Launch began with one question: what if you threw with your real arm, and the phone was the thing you threw?

## Reading a throw

The phone doesn't know it's being thrown, only how it's accelerating. So the game watches the motion sensors 100 times a second and splits a throw into stages:

1. **Hold still** for a quarter of a second. Wherever the back of the phone points becomes "forward".
2. **Swing.** The acceleration, with gravity removed, is turned into a 3D velocity, so the ball in your hand moves the way your hand does.
3. **Commit.** A hard enough push for long enough arms the throw.
4. **Let go.** The ball leaves your hand the instant you stop pushing it forward.

Whatever velocity your hand had at that moment, including forward, up and sideways, is the throw. You never press a button.

## Three things to throw

- **A ping-pong ball** for cups, ramps, windows and a bank shot off a glass panel.
- **A toy cat** whose head, legs and tail are little springs driven by the forces it feels. It dangles while you hold it, floats in free fall, spreads its legs and twists to land on its feet.
- **Knives** for a carnival target, a bullseye, balloons and a turning wheel.

Fifteen levels so far, and every one is checked by a physics sweep that throws thousands of virtual throws at it to make sure it can be won but isn't trivial.

## Finding the look

Before any final art, the same scene was painted in three styles: *Pocket Toybox*, *Kinetic Comic* and *Clockwork Carnival*. The carnival won: a lantern-lit wooden booth, a jointed wooden cat, and painted stars on every panel. From there the cat went from a concept painting to a four-view turnaround to a 3D model, and the booth's props and textures followed.

## Next
